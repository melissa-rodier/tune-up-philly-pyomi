Orchestra of Peers

A spring campaign telling the Tune Up Philly story through the students, alumni and teaching artists who live it.

A spec writing sample and campaign plan by Melissa Rodier for the Director of Marketing & Communications role at PYO Music Institute

The feature

My writing sample, built to run on the PYOMI website and anchor the campaign in the weeks before the Festival Concert.

Playing Time

A look at the 6- to 14-year-olds who are playing in a new way, as they prepare for Tune Up Philly’s spring Festival Concert

In a music room in Philadelphia, there’s a cacophony of sound before a single student has even lifted an instrument. Chairs scrape, kids chatter and laugh, and instrument cases click open. It’s the soundtrack of a Wednesday afternoon on Wissahickon Avenue, in a room buzzing with enthusiasm, until it’s finally halted by a clap for attention from [instructor name].

This is the Academy at the Salvation Army Kroc Center, one of six community site partners for Tune Up Philly. The program launched in 2010 as a free after-school program for students in under-resourced neighborhoods around the city, and it’s part of PYO Music Institute (PYOMI), a nonprofit now in its 87th season that serves more than 1,000 students across the region.

The 6- to 14-year-olds in the program are perfecting their skills on strings, woodwinds and brass with the guidance of professional teaching artists, but the program is about so much more than that. Students are also learning important life skills, and it’s evident just watching the class as [a moment that shows the “orchestra of peers” idea in action, like a student helping a neighbor find their place in the music].

Every rehearsal this season has been building toward something big: the Annual Festival Concert, a chance to perform on stage at the Temple Performing Arts Center in front of an audience of family and friends. [Student quote or anecdote about what the concert means to them and why they’re excited.]

[Human interest story about one student: what brought them here, what they’ve learned, what they want now and how the program has shaped their goals.]

For many of these students, Tune Up Philly is just the first chapter of a long story. Many continue on to the Young Musicians Debut Orchestra and other PYOMI programs. [Alumni success story in music.] And even for those who don’t pursue a career in the arts, the impact of the program stays with them. [Short story from an alum who went in a different direction.]

As the music replaces the scattered sounds of the room, a mix of discipline and joy fills the space and [description of the piece being played] comes to life. Every instrument, every page of sheet music and every teaching artist in this room is here for these students thanks to the generosity of a community that believes in programs like Tune Up Philly. That support has made an impact you can see, feel and hear, and it has opened a door for many students into a world they may not have known before. For the [number of students] at the Academy today, and the 109 students Tune Up Philly served last season, the impact of this program will echo throughout their lives.

If you’d like to learn more about Tune Up Philly, or see the impact your contribution can have for students throughout Philadelphia, click here. And if you’re looking for an amazing afternoon, there’s still time to save your seat and cheer on [names of featured students] and their orchestra of peers at this year’s Annual Festival Concert!

The campaign

Tune Up Philly, told by the people in it. Every piece of content comes from students, alumni and teaching artists, and everything builds toward the spring Festival Concert with two goals: fill the seats and grow support for the program.

One content day, many pieces

A single day at a Tune Up Philly site to capture rehearsal footage, photos, student and teaching artist interviews, and testimonials. That day feeds the feature, social, email, video and the concert program.

What Tune Up Philly Meant to Me

An alumni series with short written and video stories from former students, including those who kept playing and those who took the lessons somewhere else entirely.

Standing O

A recurring social series where students give a “standing ovation” to a peer who’s doing great, in their own words. It’s the orchestra of peers idea in action.

The Sounds of Tune Up Philly

A Spotify playlist curated by students at each of the six sites, so every community gets its own sound. Easy to share, and a fun way for supporters to get to know the kids.

Email

A mix of program emails spotlighting students, teaching artists and the concert, plus one or two emails written by a student or alum, with light editing support so their voice comes through.

Paid social

  • Boost organic posts that are already taking off
  • Concert promotion in the final weeks
  • Fundraising posts tied to the alumni series

Targeting parents, past concertgoers and local arts audiences, never the students themselves.

Musician cameos

Start close to home: PYO alumni now performing with The Philadelphia Orchestra recording short “I started where you are” messages. Then a stretch ask to Philly artists who love this city, like The Roots, to record a short message or performance for the students.

Press

Pitch the feature angle to WRTI, BroadwayWorld Philadelphia and neighborhood news, with an invitation to a rehearsal and interviews with the program director and a family.

Doing it right

  • Signed media releases from parents before anything is shared
  • First names only, with no school details
  • Playlists run on PYOMI’s account with a quick content review

How we’ll know it worked

  • Concert attendance and RSVPs
  • Donations, average gift and first-time donors
  • Email opens and clicks
  • Social saves, shares and playlist follows
  • Press pickup

Timeline

About ten weeks, building to the Festival Concert like a piece building to its finale.

  1. Weeks 1–2

    Capture

    Content day at a Tune Up Philly site. Media releases, interviews, footage and alumni outreach begin.

  2. Weeks 3–5

    Introduce

    The feature runs. Alumni series and Standing O launch. Program spotlight emails start.

  3. Weeks 6–7

    Build

    Site playlists release. Musician cameos post. Press pitches go out. Boost what’s working.

  4. Weeks 8–9

    Invite

    Student-written emails, concert promotion and fundraising ads. Final push to save a seat.

  5. Concert week

    Finale

    The Festival Concert, followed by a thank-you recap with photos and a last chance to give.
